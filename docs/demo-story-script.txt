=================================================================

THE IDEA
You build ONE chat live, step by step, like a researcher would. Everyone has heard that AI is
using a lot of electricity - so the audience can follow every step without technical knowledge.

   Tour    What's on the screen ................ 20 seconds, once
   Step 1  Ask a quick question ................ a cited, verified answer in ~20 s
   Step 2  Turn it into a policy brief ......... Deep research, ~20 s
   Step 3  See how the research connects ....... key figures + citation map, ~5 s
   Step 4  Fact-check a draft .................. catches a wrong number, ~12 s

A finished copy of the same story is in the sidebar ("★ Story · AI data centers and the grid").
If any step is slow, open that chat and continue from the same step. Nobody will notice.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

SAY (while it works, ~20 s - point at the steps appearing):

SAY:
"Here's the short version first: data centers and AI are driving the first real growth in US
electricity demand in decades - and that's straining the grid and the clean energy transition.

DO: click one of the little numbers, e.g. [1].

STEP 2 - TURN IT INTO A POLICY BRIEF  (1:05 - 1:45)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
SAY:
"Now say I'm briefing a state policymaker. I need more than a paragraph."

DO: switch "Deep research" ON. Next to it, choose Report type "Issue brief".
    Type, and press Enter:

      AI data centers and the clean energy transition

SAY (while it works, ~20 s):
"Deep research breaks the topic into sub-questions and searches them all at once - like
sending three research assistants to the library at the same time."

DO: when the brief appears, point at "Deep research · Issue brief" and "Written for:
    Policymaker", then read two or three key points.
SAY:
"It's written as a policy brief: the problem, what the evidence shows, the options. Look at
these findings: grid planners have nearly DOUBLED their demand forecasts. In one market, data
center demand added 9.4 BILLION dollars to capacity costs - costs passed on to people's bills.
And only about half of the new data-center demand may be met by renewables - the rest by gas
and coal."
DO: point at the summary at the top.
SAY:
"And notice: it tells me where WRI's reports DISAGREE - on whether AI ends up helping or hurting
the transition. A good analyst would tell you that. So does this."

WHY IT MATTERS: a brief that takes an analyst days, in seconds - every line sourced.

STEP 4 - FACT-CHECK A DRAFT  (2:15 - 2:50)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
SAY:
"Last step. Say a colleague wrote this for a blog post. Before it goes out, let's check it."

DO: 1. Stay in the SAME chat as Steps 1-3.
    2. In the box at the bottom, click "Fact-check" (next to "Ask"). The box's grey hint text
       changes to "Paste a draft, email or proposal..." - that's how you know it's switched.
    3. Paste this exact text into the box and press Enter (~12 s):

    ----- copy from here -----
    AI data centers already use most of America's electricity growth, and a PJM study found that data center load added $94 billion to capacity costs in 2024. WRI expects renewables to supply all new data center demand, and grid planners have nearly doubled their five-year demand forecasts.
    ----- to here -----

    (The same text is also at the very bottom of this file.)

SAY (while it checks):
"I've hidden four claims in this paragraph. Some are right, some aren't. Let's see what WRI's
research says about each."

DO: when the result appears, point at each claim and its verdict, in this order:

    CLAIM IN THE DRAFT                           VERDICT        SAY
    "$94 billion" added to capacity costs ...... CONTRADICTED   "WRI's figure is $9.4 billion -
                                                                 a misplaced decimal."
    "use most of America's electricity growth" . NOT FOUND      "WRI lists data centers as one of
                                                                 several drivers - not most of it."
    "renewables to supply ALL new demand" ...... NOT FOUND      "WRI never says that - its sources
                                                                 say only about half."
    "grid planners nearly doubled forecasts" ... SUPPORTED      "This one is right - and here's
                                                                 the source."

SAY (to wrap up the step):
"It caught the misplaced decimal and two claims WRI never made - and confirmed the one that's
right, with the source. This is the check you'd want before anything reaches a minister, a
journalist or a funder."

WHY IT MATTERS: protects WRI's credibility - nothing goes out in WRI's name that WRI didn't say.
