# AskWRI: user guide

AskWRI is a research assistant for the World Resources Institute. You ask a question in plain
language, and it answers only from WRI's own publications. Every claim in an answer is checked
against the page it came from, and you can open that page in one click. If WRI's research
doesn't answer your question, AskWRI says so rather than guess.

This guide is for anyone using AskWRI. You don't need any technical knowledge.

| What | Link |

Use a recent Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox. AskWRI also works on a phone.

## Getting access and signing in

AskWRI is invitation only: nobody can sign themselves up.

1. **Ask for an account.** An administrator adds your email address. You then receive an email
   from `no-reply@verificationemail.com` with a temporary password. Check your spam folder if
   it doesn't arrive within a few minutes.
2. **Open AskWRI** and click **Sign in**. You're taken to the AskWRI sign-in page.
3. **Enter your email and the temporary password.** You're asked to choose your own password:
   at least 12 characters, with an upper-case letter, a lower-case letter and a number.
4. You're taken back to AskWRI, signed in. Your email shows at the top right, with **Sign out**
   next to it.

You stay signed in for about 12 hours. After that, AskWRI asks you to sign in again.

Forgot your password? On the sign-in page, choose the forgotten-password link and follow the
email. If that doesn't work, ask an administrator to reset your account.

## The screen at a glance

**Left sidebar**

- **New chat** starts a fresh conversation.
- **Spaces** are WRI's topics: Business, Cities, Climate, Economics, Energy, Equity, Finance,
  Food, Forests, Freshwater and Ocean. The number next to each is how many publications it
  holds. Inside a space, every question searches that topic's publications only. **All of WRI**
  searches everything.
- **Your chats** for the space you're in, newest first. Click one to reopen it: the box at the
  bottom goes back to how that chat was asked (Ask, Fact-check or Explore, Deep research and its
  report type).
- **Make the sidebar wider** by dragging its right edge, so long chat titles are easier to read.
  Double-click the edge to put it back.

**The box at the bottom** is where you ask. Below it:

- **Ask**, **Fact-check** or **Explore**: three ways to use AskWRI, explained below.
- **Deep research**: turn it on for a fuller report instead of a short answer, and choose the
  **Report type**.
- **Lens**: read every publication through your own questions (explained below).
- **Filters**: limit the search to a starting year, a type of publication (report, working
  paper, issue brief…) or a region or country.

**The top bar**

- **Audience**: who answers are written for. **Auto** (the default) works it out from your
  question; you can also choose Researcher, Policymaker or Partnerships.
- **Language**: the language answers are written in (English, Español, Português, Bahasa
  Indonesia). If you ask in another language, AskWRI answers in that language anyway.
- **Download**: take the latest answer away (see "Keeping and sharing your work").

## Asking a question

Type your question the way you would ask a colleague, and press Enter. For example:

- How should civil society track adaptation finance?
- What has WRI found about electric bus adoption in cities?
- What did WRI publish in 2025 on mangroves?

While AskWRI works, you can watch its steps: what it searches, which publications it finds,
and how it checks the answer. A short answer usually takes 15 to 25 seconds.

To ask about what you just read, simply ask a follow-up in the same chat: "what about Kenya?",
"make it shorter", "now in Spanish". AskWRI remembers the conversation.

## Reading an answer

- **Summary**: two or three sentences at the top that bring the publications together.
- **The answer**: every sentence ends with numbers like **[1]**: these are its sources. Click a
  number to see the source.
- **"8 of 8 claims verified"**: every claim was checked against the passage it cites. Any
  sentence the sources didn't support was removed before you saw it. Click the line to see what
  was removed.
- **"Written for: Researcher"** (or Policymaker, Partnerships): who the answer was written for.
  Click it to have the same answer rewritten for another reader.
- **Possible implications**: AskWRI's own interpretation, clearly marked as not WRI's finding.
- **What this can't tell you**: the limits of the evidence found.
- **Questions to explore**: suggested follow-ups. Clicking one puts it in the box so you can edit
  it before asking.
- **Sources**: the list of publications used. Open it to see each one's title, type, year and
  page.

### Opening a source

Click a source (or a number in the answer). The panel on the right opens the **actual WRI PDF
at the cited page, with the passage highlighted in yellow**. It also shows the publication's
topics, and WRI's own summary of it where WRI has one. You can download the PDF from there.

### Visuals

Click **Visuals** under an answer to see:

- **Key figures**: the important numbers from the sources, quoted word for word.
- **Citation map**: the publications used, arranged by year, with arrows showing which report
  builds on, extends or uses data from which. Click a report to see the exact sentence where
  another report cites it, and open that page. Options above the map:
  - **Reports beyond the answer**: also show other WRI reports connected to these.
  - **Only linked reports**: hide reports with no connections.
  - **Show mentions too**: also show reports that are only listed in a bibliography.
  - **Download links (CSV)**: the connections as a spreadsheet, for a literature review.
  - An **Updated** label means a newer WRI report has updated this one: check the newer one
    before quoting its figures.
- **Evidence matrix**: which publication supports which point of the answer.

Click **Expand** on any visual to open it full screen. In the citation map, **Fit to window**
shows the whole map at once; **Actual size** makes it easier to read.

## The three tools

### Ask (the default)

A cited answer to your question, as described above. Turn on **Deep research** for a fuller
report and choose its **Report type**:

| Report type | Best for |
|---|---|
| Talking points | Short points for a partner or funder meeting |
| Issue brief | A policymaker: the problem, what the evidence shows, the options |
| Researcher summary | Precise findings, methods and caveats |
| Topic dossier | Comparing up to 8 papers side by side: where they agree, where they differ, what's missing |

Deep research takes 25 to 60 seconds. After a report, you can change the report type and AskWRI
rewrites it from the same sources, without searching again.

### Fact-check

Paste a draft (a paragraph, an email, a blog post, a funding proposal). AskWRI checks each claim
against WRI's publications and marks it:

- **Supported**: WRI's research says this, with the source.
- **Contradicted**: WRI's research says something different (for example, a different figure),
  with the passage and suggested wording.
- **Outdated**: WRI said this once, but a newer WRI publication has a newer figure.
- **Not found**: nothing in WRI's publications supports it.

Turn on **Deep research** for a more thorough check.

### Explore

Type a topic (for example, *adaptation finance*) instead of a question. AskWRI shows the topic's
publications over time, how they cite one another, key figures, and a short summary: a quick
way to understand a topic's history at WRI.

## Research lens

A lens lets you read every publication through the questions you care about. Click **Lens**,
write your goal (for example, *design a heat action plan*) and up to four aspects (for example,
*cost, who pays, evidence of impact*). Your next answers include a table: one row per
publication, one column per aspect, each cell summarised and cited. Click **Lens** again to
change or remove it.

## When AskWRI says "I couldn't find WRI evidence"

AskWRI declines rather than guess when WRI's publications don't answer the question, or when a
question assumes something WRI never concluded. It shows what it searched and the closest
related WRI work. You can:

- **Rephrase** the question: more specific usually works better ("cool roofs in Indian cities"
  rather than "heat").
- Inside a space: click **Search all of WRI instead** to look beyond that topic.
- Try **Deep research**, which searches more widely.

## Keeping and sharing your work

- **Download** (top bar) takes the latest answer away as **Markdown**, its **sources as CSV** or
  **BibTeX** (for reference managers), or copies it as Markdown.
- Under each answer: **Copy**, **Download answer** (or report), and **Translate**.
- Your chats are kept **in this browser only**. They aren't shared with anyone, and they
  aren't available on another computer or in another browser. **Clear** at the bottom of the
  sidebar deletes them. A private (Incognito) window forgets them when it's closed.

## Good to know

- AskWRI only knows what's in its collection: 580 WRI publications from 2014 to 2026 (365
  research publications are searched by default; requests for proposals and newsletters are
  only searched when you pick that type in Filters). If something isn't in the collection, it
  can't answer from it.
- Answers are generated. AskWRI checks every claim against its source, but always open the
  cited page before relying on a figure for something important.
- Machine-translated answers are labelled. Sources always stay in their original language so
  citations remain exact.
- Several people can use AskWRI at the same time. In one browser tab, wait for an answer to
  finish before starting a new chat: starting a new one stops the answer in progress.

## For administrators

**The admin page** (https://d17gz0gh5g00t3.cloudfront.net/admin/, or the **Admin** button at the
top right) is only available to accounts in the admins group. It shows:

- **Quality scorecard**: the latest evaluation, measured against WRI's success criteria, each
  marked On target or Below target:
  - **Citation coverage**: answers with every figure cited (target 100%).
  - **Retrieval recall@5**: how often the right WRI source is among the top five found (target
    75% or more). **Retrieval precision@5**: how many of the top five are relevant.
  - **Abstention accuracy**: questions WRI's research can't answer that AskWRI correctly declined
    (target: all of them).
  - **Median latency**: how long a Quick answer and a Deep research report take (target: under a
    minute).
- **Model-graded quality · AgentCore Evaluations**: independent model graders score every test
  answer for faithfulness to its sources, correctness, relevance and helpfulness.
- **Cost and usage**: estimated cost per answer, projected cost per 1,000 answers, tokens, 30-day
  spend against the budget, daily spend, cost by request type and by pipeline step, and the
  highest-cost requests. Click one to review it: the question, the answer, the claims that
  verification removed and the sources.
- **Live quality signals**: unsupported claims removed per answer, and how often AskWRI declines.

Use **Traffic** to switch between real users and evaluation runs, and **Window** for the last
24 hours, 7 days or 30 days.

**Adding a person** is done by the technical team (see below). Send them the person's email and
whether they should be an admin.

**Preparing a demo:** sign in, then open https://d17gz0gh5g00t3.cloudfront.net/?demo=load in the
same browser window. It replaces that browser's chats with eleven ready-made example chats:
**"★ Story · AI data centers and the grid"** (one topic followed from a quick answer to a policy
brief, its citation map and a fact-check), then one chat per feature. Use a private (Incognito)
window and keep it open: closing it forgets the chats. Demo scripts: `docs/demo-story-script.txt`
(the 3-minute story on AI data centers) and `docs/demo-script.md` (one example per feature).

## For the technical team

Everything is on the `release-branch` branch. The technical setup, commands and AWS resources
are in [README.md](README.md). The tasks you'll be asked for most:

- **Add a person** (they receive the invitation email):
  ```sh
  POOL=$(terraform -chdir=infra output -raw user_pool_id)
  aws cognito-idp admin-create-user --user-pool-id "$POOL" --username person@example.org \
    --user-attributes Name=email,Value=person@example.org Name=email_verified,Value=true
  # admins only:
  aws cognito-idp admin-add-user-to-group --user-pool-id "$POOL" --username person@example.org \
    --group-name askwri-admins
  ```
- **Publish a new version of the website:** `scripts/deploy-web.sh` (after any infrastructure
  change has been applied with Terraform).
- **Refresh the demo chats:** `uv run python scripts/record_demo_chats.py > web/public/demo-chats.json`,
  then publish the website.
- **Run the evaluation:** see `docs/evaluation-spec.md`.
